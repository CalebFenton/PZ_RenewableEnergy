description=Keep the lights on after the grid and the gas are gone, with what a Kentucky farm, cabin or RV owner could have had in 1993: solar panels, wind chargers, a treadmill, a bank of batteries, and a generator that runs on manure gas or wood. You start with a car battery in a wooden box, enough for the TV and a lamp. Every panel after that is one you found, fixed or built, every battery wears out, and the weather decides what you get.
description=
description=[b]Build 42.20 or newer.[/b] Single player and multiplayer. Fine to add to a save in progress.

description=[h2]Everything you can find or build[/h2]
description=[img]https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalebFenton/PZ_RenewableEnergy/master/art/promo/images/items.png[/img]
description=
description=[list]
description=[*][b]Banks[/b] hold the batteries and what you wire into them: inverters, charge controllers, a charger and a [b]Solar Failsafe[/b] that starts the generator when they run low.
description=[*][b]Batteries wear[/b] with every deep drain. Service and recondition them, each repair bringing them back less far, then strip and rebuild them with plates you cast yourself.
description=[*][b]Panels follow the sun[/b]: flat ones for summer, wall panels for winter, ground racks for the year. Storms crack modules; seal them with epoxy or glue, or reglaze them.
description=[*][b]Wind chargers, treadmills, digesters and gasifiers[/b] fill in when the sun won't.
description=[*][b]Small gadgets[/b] need no bank: a maintainer for a parked car's battery, a charger for flashlight batteries, and garden lights.
description=[/list]

description=[h2]Getting started[/h2]
description=[img]https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalebFenton/PZ_RenewableEnergy/master/art/promo/images/bank.png[/img]
description=
description=[olist]
description=[*]Craft a battery box and [b]Place Object[/b] indoors.
description=[*]Right-click it and [b]Wire In[/b] batteries and an inverter.
description=[*][b]Connect Bank[/b], like a generator: Electricity 3, or the generator magazine.
description=[*]Put up a panel outside, then [b]Connect Sources in Reach[/b].
description=[*][b]Turn On[/b]. [b]Status[/b] shows what it's doing, and why each source isn't at full output.
description=[/olist]
description=
description=The bank powers everything a generator in its spot would, the neighbours' lights included, so set up away from town. Switch on more than the inverters carry and the power cuts out until you switch something off.
description=
description=[h2]Wind[/h2]
description=[img]https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalebFenton/PZ_RenewableEnergy/master/art/promo/images/wind.png[/img]
description=
description=Build them on the [b]Welding[/b] page. A scoop rotor turns in any breeze and never breaks. A treadmill's motor, a car's alternator or a homemade alternator on a mast makes real power, the homebuilt turbine twice the treadmill's. Put them up high, clear of trees and buildings and four squares from each other, or they steal each other's wind.
description=
description=When a gale sets the blades creaking and rattling, [b]Set the Brake[/b] or you'll be replacing them: carved, cut from pipe, or forged steel that breaks last. The turbine swings itself out of the worst of it.
description=
description=[h2]Treadmills[/h2]
description=[img]https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalebFenton/PZ_RenewableEnergy/master/art/promo/images/treadmill.png[/img]
description=
description=No sun, no wind? [b]Wire the Motor[/b], connect the treadmill to a bank, and [b]Run on Treadmill[/b]: half an hour banks about what a fridge-freezer draws in that time, at the cost of endurance. Taken apart, it gives up the motor wind chargers are built around.
description=
description=[h2]Biogas[/h2]
description=[img]https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalebFenton/PZ_RenewableEnergy/master/art/promo/images/biogas.png[/img]
description=
description=Feed a digester manure and scraps, keep it above 10 °C, and it makes gas. Overfeed it and it sours until you add wood ash or baking soda; indoors, it can poison the house, and it stinks. [b]Install Gas Conversion Kit[/b] on a generator, [b]Connect Gas Line[/b] with a rubber hose for every five squares, and [b]Run on Gas[/b]: its tank stays as it was. A tire pump or a fridge compressor bottles the gas for propane tanks.
description=
description=[h2]Wood gas[/h2]
description=[img]https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalebFenton/PZ_RenewableEnergy/master/art/promo/images/woodgas.png[/img]
description=
description=FEMA's 1989 plans run an engine on wood: a gasifier from a trash can, a steel drum or welded sheet metal, right beside a kitted generator. Fill it with wood or charcoal and light it, clean out the ash, change the filter packing before the tar gets through, and keep it outdoors.
description=
description=[h2]Crafting[/h2]
description=Learn recipes from the Homestead Power magazines and FEMA's plans, by levelling the skills they use, or by researching a part you found. Most need a table and enough light; battery rebuilds need a furnace, a hand press and a kiln. Inverters, the maintainer and the battery charger can't be crafted. Every recipe is on [url=https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalebFenton/PZ_RenewableEnergy/master/art/promo/images/recipes.png]one sheet[/url].
description=
description=[h2]Where to look[/h2]
description=Think about who had this stuff in 1993: campers and boaters, farmers, electricians, the phone and power companies, road crews and preppers. Cracked modules are common, good ones aren't. Some places come already set up, their panels usually up on the roof where the sun is, so bring what it takes to build your own way up. Now and then a barn or a hunting shack has something out back, never in working order.
