title=Renewable Energy
description=Sooner or later the grid goes down, and sooner or later the gas runs out too. A generator buys you time, but it's loud, it fumes up the house, and it eats scrap electronics to keep going.
description=
description=This mod is the other option: the solar gear a Kentucky household, farm or RV owner could have owned in 1993. You start with a car battery in a wooden box and an inverter that plugs into a lighter socket, enough for the TV, your tapes and a lamp. A few months later there's a room full of L-16s, a yard of panels, and a generator you only start when it's been cloudy all week.
description=
description=[b]Build 42.20 or newer.[/b]
description=
description=[h2]How real is it?[/h2]
description=Real where that's fun, gamey where it isn't. The sun moves the way it does over Knox County, worked out from the in-game date and time and checked against sunlight measured at Louisville. By December a flat panel makes less than a third of what it did in July. Clouds, fog and rain cut it, and snow piles up on flat panels until you sweep it off. Batteries wear faster the harder you drain them.
description=
description=An inverter is just a number of watts, though. Go over it and the bank shuts off until you switch something off. Panels make two and a half times their real output by default (there's a slider), and sixteen calculators make a homemade panel.
description=
description=[h2]How it plays[/h2]
description=[list]
description=[*]Before the grid goes, fill your car batteries on a car battery charger, or wire the charger into a battery box and let the grid fill it.
description=[*]The first weeks: a battery box, two car batteries and a Plug-in Inverter run the TV and a lamp, or a mini fridge.
description=[*]Weeks 3 to 6: a battery rack of deep cycle batteries and a 600 W inverter run the kitchen fridge.
description=[*]Months 2 and 3: a battery bank of L-16s, an Inverter/Charger and a failsafe on the generator run the fridge and freezer all year.
description=[*]After that: two Inverter/Chargers in a metal bank run the electric range and the dryer.
description=[/list]
description=Battery boxes, racks and banks come in wood or metal, go indoors, and every part has to be wired in with tools and wire. Run the generator a couple of hours through the bank's chargers on a grey day, then live on the batteries.
description=
description=[h2]Panels[/h2]
description=Ground racks hold two modules and make the most over a year. Flat panels are great in summer and bad in winter. Wall panels beat flat ones all winter and never hold snow. Storms crack modules now and then, and you fix them with glass and epoxy. Take apart calculators, garden lights and old solar gadgets for their cells and you can build your own.
description=
description=[h2]Keeping it running[/h2]
description=Service a worn battery with water and baking soda, recondition it, or strip it down, recast its plates at a furnace and rebuild it like new. Inverters and controllers wear and take scrap electronics to fix, like a generator.
description=
description=[h2]Finding it[/h2]
description=Look where people had this stuff in 1993: campers, auto parts stores, farms, electricians' vans, the phone and power companies, and preppers' caches. Some storage rooms hold a whole RV solar kit, and cracked modules turn up in garages, sheds and junkyards. There are solar sites to raid: stock tank pumps in the pastures, farmhouses, campers, the phone company's relay sheds, ranger stations, the Deer Valley Power yard, and solar arrow board trailers at road works and in highway pileups. Some houses have solar garden lights out front that still work.
